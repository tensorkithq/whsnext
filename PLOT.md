# Marketing & Plot Brief — Realtime Audience-Controlled Stories

## 1. Product Concept

A live story where everybody watching controls what happens next.

One protagonist.

One shared timeline.

Audience votes.

Winning decision becomes canon.

Story immediately continues.

Core positioning:

**You don't watch the story. You decide it.**

---

## 2. Format

Mobile-first vertical video.

Visually and behaviorally familiar to TikTok:

* full-screen protagonist video
* comments
* reactions
* vote overlay
* live percentages
* countdown
* shareable moments
* next episode countdown

Difference:

Audience is not commenting from sidelines.

**Comments and votes change the plot.**

---

## 3. Launch Property

# LAGOS WAHALA

Interactive Nigerian-life comedy built around ordinary situations escalating into increasingly ridiculous problems.

Tone:

* recognizable
* fast
* culturally specific
* chaotic
* funny because situations feel possible
* protagonist continuously forced into imperfect choices

Core tension:

**There is rarely a correct answer. Audience chooses which problem protagonist should create next.**

---

## 4. Episode Structure

Each episode begins with clear premise.

Example:

### Episode 1 — Salary Just Entered

Salary enters.

Immediately:

* landlord wants money
* mother wants attention
* friend has business proposal
* work commute starts
* relationship complications emerge

Audience controls protagonist through escalating consequences.

Story ends with:

* outcome
* character status
* money/resources
* relationships
* unresolved problems
* audience-created personality/archetype

---

## 5. Plot Principle

Every choice should satisfy three conditions:

**1. Immediately understandable**

Viewer should vote without needing explanation.

**2. Socially debatable**

Different viewers should genuinely prefer different choices.

**3. Consequential**

Choice must alter future scenes, relationships, resources, or problems.

Avoid:

* obviously correct option
* obviously stupid option
* cosmetic choices
* choices whose consequences disappear immediately

---

## 6. Story Rhythm

Each segment should contain:

```text
setup
↓
new problem
↓
escalation
↓
decision
↓
audience vote
↓
consequence
```

Bridge scenes remain narrative content.

Useful bridges:

* protagonist walking somewhere
* entering vehicle
* waiting for someone
* answering phone
* reaction shot
* elevator ride
* ordering something
* opening message
* knocking at door
* travelling between locations
* awkward silence
* establishing location

Bridge should preserve momentum while remaining compatible with generated continuation.

## 8. Launch Cadence

**5 episodes/day.**

Each episode is an appointment.

Product should show:

**NEXT STORY STARTS IN: 02:14:32**

This creates repeated daily return moments rather than infinite passive feed consumption.

Proposed programming model:

```text
Morning
Midday
Afternoon
Evening
Late night
```

Exact times remain TBD based on audience behavior.

---

## 9. Why Five Episodes

Five gives enough frequency for:

* users missing one session to catch another
* different plots across dayparts
* experimentation
* social sharing between episodes
* countdown-driven return behavior

Without making stories feel permanently available.

Live participation should have value.

Someone watching replay can see what audience chose, but cannot change history.

---

## 10. Core Marketing Hook

Primary:

**What happens next is up to everyone watching.**

Other usable directions:

**Nigeria's first story controlled live by the internet.**

**Thousands of people. One protagonist. Terrible decisions.**

**You have 10 seconds. Decide what happens next.**

## 11. Social Distribution

Every major decision can become standalone social content:

```text
THE INTERNET HAD 10 SECONDS TO CHOOSE:

A. Pay landlord
B. Call his mother
C. Invest ₦50k
```

Then:

**62% chose C.**

Follow with consequence clip.

This creates content native to TikTok, Reels, X, and Shorts without requiring separate narratives.

---

## 13. Audience Psychology

Product should optimize for:

* curiosity: what will winning choice cause?
* agency: my vote matters
* conflict: why are people voting for that?
* urgency: only seconds to choose
* collective identity: “we did this”
* regret: audience sees consequences of its own decision
* anticipation: next episode countdown
* FOMO: story progresses whether individual viewer is present or not

---

## 14. Protagonist Design

Protagonist should be recognizable enough for viewers to care but flexible enough for audience control.

Needs:

* clear personality
* expressive reactions
* recurring relationships
* understandable motivations
* flaws
* consequences that persist

Audience should gradually develop relationship with protagonist.

Goal:

**People return not only for format, but to see what they have done to this person's life.**

---

## 15. Plot Engine Rule

Celeris should not treat each scene as isolated comedy.

Episode must maintain:

* escalating stakes
* callbacks
* unresolved problems
* character memory
* consequences
* resource depletion/gain
* emotional continuity
* setup/payoff

Early choices should be capable of resurfacing later.

Example:

Ignoring landlord at minute 2 may create confrontation at minute 12.

---

## 16. Launch Content Strategy

Start narrow.

Primary franchise:

**Lagos Wahala**

Build recognizable protagonist and world before expanding into many unrelated formats.

Initial episodes can vary premise while maintaining same cultural universe.

Example launch slate:

1. Salary Just Entered
2. First Date Wahala
3. Your Mother Is Coming Over
4. Your Boss Calls on Saturday
5. Detty December With ₦40k Left

Use audience response to identify which situations generate strongest voting disagreement and retention.

---

## 17. Launch Success Signals

Measure:

* live viewers per episode
* vote participation rate
* votes per decision
* retention through episode
* viewers returning for next episode
* comments per viewer
* shares
* percentage joining before countdown ends
* most contested choices
* replay views
* daily episodes watched per user

Most important early signal:

**Do viewers return specifically because they want to influence what happens next?**
